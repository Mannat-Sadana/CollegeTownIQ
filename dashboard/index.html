How to read CollegeTownIQ

   

      The map is designed to reveal tradeoffs rather than produce a single       “best neighborhood” score. Housing cost, transit accessibility, food       access, and transportation characteristics are intentionally presented       as separate dimensions so users can examine how they interact.        

      This allows the analysis to ask a more useful question:       how does the practical burden of living in a college town change       depending on where someone lives?

What the results suggest

The estimated negative association between transit distance and rent weakens after renter share is included. With both additional controls, the estimate is smaller and statistically insignificant. Housing tenure may partly explain the original relationship, but this analysis does not establish causality.

Limitations: small complete-case sample (n = 25), observational data, and an imperfect college-enrollment proxy. Coefficients represent estimated differences in median gross rent per additional kilometer of transit distance, holding included controls constant.
